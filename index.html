a cozy research collective

We fancy ourselves a cozy research boutique of scholars specializing in the entanglements of media, communication technology, and human behavior. Our orientation says the most valuable research strives for validity and precision without sacrificing warmth, personality, and va-va-voom.

Get to Know Us

Our goal, quite unapologetically, is to create fresh scientific insights about communication technologies by bringing warmth, rigor, and playfulness to our research projects. In our philosophy, whimsy and creativity sit happily alongside statistics and meticulous measurement. We have a fondness for the artisanal qualities of doing scientific research and think they ought to be celebrated, with much jubilation.
Good research deserves a bit of pizzazz, and we’re happy to supply it.

Our interests

There is a nearly endless source of inspiration from the mysteries of communication, technology, and human behavior. Media and communication technologies shape how we think, feel, and connect, and our behaviors shape them back. We care deeply about both directions of this social process. Our research interests draw our imaginations towards friendships, families and the small, everyday rituals through which connection and identity take form. Oftentimes, our answers look like a kaleidoscope, rather than a tidy APA-formatted list of takeaways, and honestly, we prefer it that way. The dazzling chaos of human communication and technology keeps us interested and inspired.

Our style

Forget lab coats, beakers, and p-values. Think more of a dandy corner boutique where you discover magnificent little treasures. We’re a collective, because research really is best done with others, especially over a cappuccino. Slow, collaborative research is our preferred mode. Ideas find their nerve and their charm, and frankly become more interesting, when they are debated with affection. In our minds, hard-hitting questions about your research are a form of care. We take theory, method, and data very seriously, working ferociously to achieve our personal research goals. But we must confess that we wouldn’t dare strive for perfection; the seams showing make the findings far more interesting, if you ask us.

Our invitation

Although we are a small, curated group of academic researchers, we welcome others into our little boutique with great excitement. We seek connections to scholars and students, engineers and designers, journalists, and many others. We walk around our building infamously confused, so we still have oodles to learn from others. The University of Illinois Chicago Department of Communication is our home. We are located just outside of the Loop in Chicago and invite you to stop by when traveling through our great city. By some miracle, if you have grant money to share, consider us your captive audience.

We’d Love to Help Out

Here you will find tools created by members of the medialoop collective. They are free to use. If you have questions about how to use them, email us.

Survey Measures01 / 06Survey MeasuresOur primary survey measure is the Perceived Algorithm Responsiveness and Insensitivity Scale. This 15-item scale measures two variables: perceived algorithm responsiveness (PAR) and perceived algorithm insensitivity (PAI). It is validated for Facebook, Twitter, TikTok and Instagram; more information is in our Social Media + Society publication.Get the scale Open Science Materials02 / 06Open Science MaterialsFor many studies you can find the preregistration, survey or experiment materials, data and code on the Open Science Framework. If you are looking for a specific scale or procedure used in our research, start here. If it doesn’t make sense, we can answer any questions.Go to OSF Social Media Simulation03 / 06Social Media SimulationTruman is a fake social networking site where participants are randomly assigned to conditions when they sign up, and interact with pre-programmed bots the researcher controls. Everything needed to replicate our experiments or run your own is on GitHub. Using Truman takes technical skills, especially JavaScript; more is in our CHI and CSCW papers.View on GitHub Conference Presentations04 / 06Conference PresentationsA folder of slides from past conference presentations, colloquiums and job talks. Use these as ideas.Open the folder Poster Presentations05 / 06Poster PresentationsA few poster presentations to serve as inspiration for others. Less is more here.
